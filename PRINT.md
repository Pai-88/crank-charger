# Print and build

Everything here is generated by `cad/planetary.py` from `design.transmission()`.
Run it again after any change; it refuses to export if a check fails.

## Print order

| # | file (`cad/stl/`) | orientation | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `coupon_pair_print_in_place`, `coupon_plate` | flat | about an hour. Two gears printed already in mesh. If they will not come apart, do NOT print the gearset: say so, and `PIP_BACKLASH` in `cad/planetary.py` goes up again |

| 4 | `wheel` | back on the bed, post up | 236 mm across, 89 mm tall |
| 5 | `frame` | as exported: front face on the bed | 210 x 225 on the plate, 93 mm tall |
| 6 | `batches/plate5_washers` | flat | 12 M8 washers + the M6 penny washer, 1.6 mm, 13 bodies, about 10 minutes; replaces the bought washers (2026-10-01) |

The same parts packed onto four full P1S plates, one STL per print job, are in
`cad/stl/batches/` (`plate1_coupon_and_small_parts`, `plate2_gearset`, `plate3_wheel`,
`plate4_frame`). `cad/batches.py` rebuilds them and checks spacing, the plate edge and
the printer's reserved front-left corner.

## Slicer settings (Bambu Studio), after the first gearset came out as spaghetti

Start from a fresh project (File, New), not the RL car plate: that carried its
own settings. Then set these; everything else stays at the Bambu default.

| where | setting | value |
|---|---|---|
| Filament | material | plain PLA, or PETG with the printer's PETG preset (temperatures and fan from the preset). **Not "Support for PLA"**, which the AMS showed in slot 2 on 30 Sep |
| Process, Quality | avoid crossing walls | **on**. The ring's inside is a 340 mm perimeter of teeth; every travel across it drags a string in PETG |
| Process, Quality | seam position | random, so the seams do not all land on one tooth |
| Process, Quality | layer height | **0.2 mm** (0.28 is what printed the spaghetti) |
| Process, Quality | initial layer height | 0.2 mm |
| Process, Quality | elephant foot compensation | 0.2 mm |
| Process, Strength | wall loops | 4 |
| Process, Strength | sparse infill | 25 %, gyroid |
| Process, Speed | outer wall | 80 mm/s for PLA, 50 mm/s for PETG |
| Process, Speed | inner wall | 120 mm/s |
| Process, Speed | overhang slow down | on (the default) |
| Process, Support | enable support | off |
| Process, Others | skirt loops | 0 |
| Process, Others | brim | none |
| Filament, Cooling | part cooling fan | 100 % from layer 3 (the default for PLA) |

What changed in the files themselves, so the print is less sensitive to all of
the above: every flank in the gearset and the coupon now stands 0.28 mm from its
neighbour instead of 0.19, and the bottom 0.6 mm of every tooth is shaved back
0.25 mm, so a squashed first layer cannot bridge the gap. Wheel and frame are
unchanged.

The order still stands: plate 1 first. If the coupon gears do not pull apart,
stop and say so.

## The ring on its own: the split ring (plates 2a and 2b)

A one-piece herringbone ring cannot be added to planets and a sun that already
exist: herringbone teeth only go together sideways, and inside a ring there is
no sideways. So the ring-alone version is TWO rings, each a plain helix half as
tall, which screw onto the gears one from each side and bolt together through
six lugs. Same teeth, same dogs, same skirt. Use it when the planets and sun
from a gearset print are good and only the ring is not.

| plate | file | orientation | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2a | `ring_rear_half` | as exported | 6 mm tall, 248 mm across the lugs |
| 2b | `ring_front_half` | as exported, split face on the bed | carries the skirt and dogs |

Extra hardware: **6 x M3 x 10 screws**. They cut their own thread in the front
half's lugs; do not overtighten.

Assembly of the split ring, before step 5 of the main sequence:

1. Rear half on the bench, lugs down, counterbores underneath.
2. Sun in the middle. Screw each planet down into the ring: it turns as it goes,
   because a helix is a thread. The planet must mesh the sun at the same time.
3. Front half on top, skirt up. Turn it until its teeth drop onto the planets'
   upper halves and it screws down flat onto the rear half. The planets line
   the two halves up; the lugs will be over each other when the teeth are.
4. Six M3 x 10 from the back, heads in the counterbores. Snug.
5. Twist the ring against the sun: everything must turn as before.

## Assembly, in this order

1. Free the gearset: twist the ring against the sun until all five parts turn.
   They stay interlocked. That is the herringbone doing its job; do not try to
   pull them apart.
2. Press a 608 into the front of each planet (the side that faced up on the
   printer). Press one 608 into the back of the wheel hub, drop the hub spacer
   in from the front, press the second 608 in on top.
3. Motor onto the back of the wall: four M5 bolts from the motor side straight
   into the 4.3 mm holes. Drive them slowly, they cut their own thread in the
   wall; stop when the motor flange is tight, do not strip them.
4. Spider: push the M8 x 40 through the middle from the BACK so its head sits in
   the pocket. Push the three M8 x 50 through from the FRONT. Two washers onto
   each of the three.
5. Lower the gearset, front face first, onto the three pins so each passes
   through a planet bearing. Then a pin spacer onto each pin, foot last.
6. Offer this whole cartridge to the wall: the three pins into their holes and
   the sun onto the motor shaft, turning the ring a little until the flat lines
   up. Washer and nyloc on each pin behind the wall. Firm.
7. Washer onto the axle, then the wheel, turning it until the six dogs on the
   ring drop into the six notches in the rim. Washer, nyloc. Snug, not tight:
   the wheel must spin freely and the ring must rattle a little under it.
8. M6 nut into the pocket in the back of the wheel (reach through a window).
   Grip over the post, disc toward the wheel. Penny washer, M6 bolt.

## Known and not hidden

- **Nothing here has been printed.** Every check that passes is a check on
  geometry, not on plastic. The print-in-place gearset is the biggest risk:
  that is what the coupon is for.
- The spokes sweep 4.5 mm in front of the fixed spider. A finger pushed through
  a window while the wheel turns gets pinched. All the gear meshes are inside
  the ring, which is safer than an open pair, but it is not guarded.
- The motor's flange thickness is assumed at 5 mm. If yours is thinner the M5
  bolts stand further out of the wall; there is 2.2 mm of margin to the planets.
- The 10 % loss per mesh in `design.py` is a guess, and this layout has two
  meshes in series.
